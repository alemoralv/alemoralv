# Daylight style notes

The visual direction follows the user's request for a lighter, more playful professional site. DESIGN.md remains historical evidence for the existing shared board. This additive stylesheet applies only to `body.daylight` on the homepage; no shared board source, note, authored biography, or reference needs rewriting.

Archivo keeps the site's recognizable voice. Wide uppercase ALEJANDRO is the opening anchor; Caveat is retained for a single friendly note and the authentic mathematical notation. The canvas is cool near-white (#f8faf6), text is deep blue-green (#20363e), and cobalt (#285ec7) consistently marks links and controls. A quiet coral detail belongs to the chance sketch and closing mark.

The independently moving studio plate sits furthest back. Thin orbital ink establishes a middle plane. The original color portrait is a slightly rotated physical print, with a subtle offset shadow and paper tape. The chance drawing is nearest. The biography and links stay fixed and immediately readable. Scroll Craft drives the distant plate; the page-local progress/pointer values drive the print and ink.

The preserved long biography needs real space. Desktop uses an asymmetric two-column introduction. Phone keeps the authored reading order in one column, with its own photographic background crop and a narrower, less-rotated print. No artificial pinning or hidden text makes this content harder to reach.

The reference sections use spacing, type and rules rather than repeated cards. Their background retains the user's exact formula writing at faint blue-pencil strength. Only the section rules grow with scroll; text is fully rendered at first paint, with JavaScript disabled, and under reduced motion. Controls have at least 44px targets and visible cobalt focus outlines.

Read before implementation: Scroll Craft SKILL.md and references/taste.md, devices.md, hero-depth.md, and approved-collection.md. Actual browser evidence is recorded separately by the root QA pass; these notes describe the design contract, not a claim of visual verification.
